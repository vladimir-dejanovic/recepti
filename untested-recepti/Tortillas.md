## Ingredients
-  360g all-purpose flour
- 6g fine sea salt
- 4g baking powder (optional, but it yields a slightly softer, puffier tortilla)
- 60g fat (traditional lard is best for flavor and texture, but vegetable shortening or softened unsalted butter work beautifully)
- 180g very warm water (about 40°C / 100°F)

## Instructions
- In a mixing bowl, whisk together the 360g of flour, 6g of salt, and 4g of baking powder.
- Add the 60g of fat to the bowl. Use your fingertips to rub the fat into the flour mixture until it resembles coarse, sandy crumbs.
- Pour in the 180g of warm water. Mix with a wooden spoon or your hands until a shaggy dough forms. Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface and knead it for 3 to 5 minutes. It should become smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky, but not stick to your fingers.
- Divide the dough into equal portions (about 45g to 50g each for standard taco-sized tortillas) and roll them into smooth balls. Place them on a tray, cover them with a damp kitchen towel or plastic wrap, and let them **rest for at least 30 minutes**. 
- Lightly dust your work surface and rolling pin with flour. Take one dough ball (keep the others covered) and roll it out from the center outwards, turning the dough a quarter-turn after each roll to keep it round. Roll it as thin as you possibly can—it should be almost translucent.
- A well-heated cast iron skillet is the absolute best tool for this. Heat the dry skillet over medium-high heat (do not add oil). Carefully lay the rolled tortilla flat into the hot pan.
Cook for 30 to 45 seconds on the first side. You will see bubbles forming on the surface and golden-brown spots underneath.
Flip it and cook for another 15 to 30 seconds on the other side.
- **As soon as the tortilla comes out of the pan, place it inside a folded clean kitchen towel or a tortilla warmer**. The trapped steam from the freshly cooked tortillas stacking on top of each other is what keeps them soft and pliable.
